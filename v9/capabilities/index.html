Capabilities

Name what you’ve got. We’ll find who wants it.

Every PPG deal starts with something your business already has, or can reach through a partner. Here are the nine ways we turn that into more profit, what you gain from each, and where it fits your business. Every one runs through the same PPG Deal Method.

Capacity and capability Customer relationships Distribution and access

The fastest place to start is what you already paid for.

Customers who already bought from you. You paid to win them once. Some stopped for reasons worth a second look.

Capacity you’re already carrying. A line, a shift, a kitchen, a warehouse or a fleet. Each one running below what it can handle.

Both start with records and assets you already have. Nothing new to build before the first test.

Size it from your own books. The accounts that went quiet, and what each one used to earn you. The hours your line sits idle, and what you pay to keep it ready.

Nine ways to grow your bottom line.

1. Strategic joint ventures and alliances

Asset group: Distribution and access

Build together what neither side can build alone.

What you gain: other companies’ resources, relationships, infrastructure and capabilities, without building everything yourself.

For example: you and another company bring customers, skills or assets that fit together. Who brings what, who owns what and who earns what are set in writing.

First check:
whether the combined value is big enough to be worth the coordination.

2. Distribution and channel partnerships

Asset group: Distribution and access

Sell through companies that already sell to your buyer.

What you gain: new customers and market access through channels other companies have already built.

For example: distributors, resellers and sales teams who already reach your buyer. Or a referral partner your buyer already trusts, with clear terms on who owns the customer and how both sides get paid.

First check:
what each side keeps after the channel takes its cut. And whether their customers actually need what you sell.

3. Underused asset monetization

Asset group: Capacity and capability

Get paid for what sits idle.

What you gain: more profit from idle capacity, inventory, intellectual property, relationships and the infrastructure you already run.

For example: your open line making product for a brand with no plant of its own, under a co-manufacturing contract. Or your equipment, space, trucks or skilled labor leased to a company that needs them.

First check:
the real cost to deliver and the quality bar. Then what it earns after the cost and the risk of sharing it.

4. Cross-selling and complementary offers

Asset group: Customer relationships

Two products. One buyer. One offer.

What you gain: more contribution profit from the customers you already serve.

For example: your product and another the same buyer needs, sold or marketed together. Their customers become yours, and yours become theirs.

First check:
whether the bundle is worth more to the buyer than the parts.

5. Geographic and market expansion

Asset group: Distribution and access

Enter a new market without building a new base there.

What you gain: entry into new markets through commercial partners, so you need less new permanent infrastructure to get there.

6. Licensing and private-label arrangements

Asset group: Capacity and capability

Get paid for what you already know how to do.

What you gain: new income streams, or a wider range to sell, without developing every product or capability yourself.

For example: your process, formula, brand, certification or know-how, used by another company in a market you don’t serve. Or private-label supply, where you make the product another brand sells.

First check:
what can be protected, and who would pay to use it.

7. Cost reduction and resource sharing

Asset group: Capacity and capability

Buy better by buying together.

What you gain: better margins through joint purchasing, shared infrastructure, new suppliers and operating partnerships.

For example: shared purchasing, a new supplier, or outsourcing work that isn’t core.

First check:
the savings after the cost of switching.

8. Dormant opportunity recovery

Asset group: Customer relationships

Bring back customers you already paid to win.

What you gain: profitable new business from neglected customers, stalled proposals and other demand nobody followed up.

For example: a win-back of your old and quiet accounts, sorted by value and by why they left. Each one gets a specific reason to come back. Outreach goes out only the way you approve, and within your consent rules.

First check:
your own order history, compared against a control group where the data allows.

9. Multi-party commercial arrangements

Asset group: Distribution and access

Connect what several businesses have.

What you gain: opportunities that only exist when the right resources from several businesses are put together.

Examples of what we pursue. Not past PPG results.
