Executive summary

The deal is the route to market, not a replacement truck

All 4 advisors, working independently, rejected the obvious framing. This is not a "find Lou a new distributor" deal. It is a deal about improving and protecting the economics of Garage's whole route to market, with AB One's absorption of Straub as the event that opened the conversation, not necessarily the transaction PPG gets paid on.

The lead structure is the Prepared Garage mandate. A signed, narrowly scoped performance mandate under which PPG builds a vetted wholesaler bench before AB One decides anything. If Garage is not selected, or is selected on terms Lou hates, PPG runs the placement. If Garage is selected, the bench becomes the credible alternative behind Lou's consent, and a 24-month standby clause applies PPG's placement terms automatically if AB One drops Garage later. It is the only structure that is useful in every branch, it costs Lou nothing unless it produces, and it is the structure Lou actually called back about.

The fee is measured against the counterfactual, or it is not collectible. Two advisors independently wrote the same formula, and the other 2 built the same idea into contract language:

PPG fee = 25% (Hypothesis) × max(0, realized contribution with PPG − agreed baseline contribution without PPG)

If Lou could have kept 80% of his Straub profit on his own and PPG gets him to 90%, PPG earned the 10 points, not the 90. That discipline is what makes the mandate signable, and it is also what caps the honest numbers below.

Successful standalone placement, revised central case $13,901 PPG year 1 fee, and $33,362 over 3 years (Hypothesis). At a 20% Straub share, replacing the model's original 30% base case of $20,851.
The bundle, where the money is $34,701 PPG year 1 if Garage anchors a portfolio with 2 recruited Straub-roster partners (Hypothesis). $83,282 over 3 years.
Expected value today ~$2,500 Probability-weighted year 1 (Hypothesis). The chance PPG earns $0 is about 79%. That is the honest shape of a first deal.

The planning numbers moved down during this process, and the report shows its work. The model's original 30% Straub share did not survive cross-examination: the Brewers Association classified Garage as a brewpub, a classification that requires at least 25% of sales on site, and Lou's taproom weight and self-distribution history point the same way. The planning range is now 15% to 20% (Estimate) until Lou gives us the real split. At a 10% share, the standalone fee falls to $6,950 (Hypothesis) and the correct posture flips to bundle-led. Tomorrow's callback exists to replace these labels with facts, starting with that one.

2
The situation

Straub, AB One, and a consent nobody has defined yet

On November 13, 2026, Anheuser-Busch announced it would acquire Straub Distributing's distribution rights for A-B brands, plus selected third-party craft and non-alcoholic brands, transferring to AB One (Sourced). The close is expected in early 2027, after which Straub becomes AB One Anaheim, operating from the same facility (Sourced). Straub sells about 9 million cases a year and employs more than 330 people (Sourced). This was Straub's choice: Straub approached Anheuser-Busch (Sourced, Straub's president to Inc.). It is AB One's second California buyback of the year, after Advance Beverage in Bakersfield (Sourced).

The mechanics that matter for Garage are 3 sentences long. Only selected brands transfer. A transfer requires the supplier's consent: the announcement language is "subject to supplier approval" (Sourced). And as of November 23, 2026, AB One has published nothing else: no selection list, no consent process, no criteria, no deadlines (verified). Nobody can tell Lou today where Garage lands. That vacuum is the timing argument for signing a mandate now, while being prepared is still cheap.

Garage's exposure is specific. Straub's core territory is Orange County plus parts of Riverside County; Garage sits in Straub's "expanded territory" subset, alongside Brewery X, Bootlegger's, Left Coast, Four Sons, Capacity, and Hurray's Girl Beer (Sourced, Straub's own site). Trade coverage treats that subset as the most exposed group in the transition, and Beer Marketer's Insights expects that "most of the remaining suppliers will look for other routes to market" (Sourced). A beverage attorney quoted by Inc. called the deal "terrible news" for Straub's craft clients and expects disputes over terminations and conflicts of interest (Sourced). The clock is roughly 6 to 14 weeks to the close (Estimate), and wholesalers allocate their 2027 craft books early. Prepared beats fast.

Consent is a negotiating position inside a reasonable, documented process. It is not a veto to wave around, and California law can make it expensive to treat it as one.

One legal fact disciplines every consent conversation in this report. Business and Professions Code § 25000.9 makes a beer manufacturer who unreasonably withholds consent to a transfer of an ownership interest in a wholesaler's business liable in damages to that wholesaler (Sourced, statute text). Garage is the manufacturer. Straub is the wholesaler. The statute points at Lou, not at AB One: unreasonably withholding consent could make Garage liable to Straub, with damages capped at compensatory damages plus costs, fair market value expressly including goodwill, and any successor's payment offsetting. The word "unreasonably" is not defined anywhere in the section. Whether the AB One structure triggers it, and what Garage's own Straub agreement requires, are counsel questions. The legal gates

None of this is legal advice. Each item is a counsel question that gates execution, in the order it bites.

  1. Section 25000.9 consent liability. Counsel determines whether the AB One structure triggers it, what Garage's Straub agreement requires, and what a reasonable, documented consent process looks like. PPG never recommends withholding or delaying consent to create leverage.
  2. PPG's own licensing question. Whether PPG's compensated distribution-related activities require a license or a specific compliant structure. Until counsel answers, PPG does not take title to beer, receive orders, handle sale proceeds, or perform functions reserved to licensees. Calling the fee a consulting fee does not settle it. This gates the whole engagement.
  3. Tied-house law. California ABC generally prohibits supplier payments or things of value to retailers outside narrow exceptions, and routing a payment through a third party does not cure it (Sourced, ABC guidance). No slotting, no referral fees to accounts, no per-drink bounties. The host programme is built as lawful co-marketing only, counsel-approved before outreach.
  4. Garage's existing Straub agreement. Assignment, termination, compensation, and territory clauses decide what placement rights exist. Consent to the AB One transfer is not the same as a right to appoint a different distributor. PPG has not seen this document. It is the gating document for Branches B and C.
  5. Portfolio coordination. The bundle involves competitors: separate mandates, separate agreements, no exchange of pricing or customer information between suppliers, counsel supervising. PPG is paid by the brands only, disclosed to the wholesaler.

The standing weaknesses
