Capabilities

Name what you’ve got. We’ll find who wants it.

Every PPG deal starts with something your business already has, or can reach through a partner. Here’s what we build, the result you want from each one, and the first thing we check. Every one runs through the same PPG Deal Method.

The fastest place to start is what you already paid for.

Customers who already bought from you. You paid to win them once. Some stopped for reasons worth a second look.

Capacity you’re already carrying. A line, a shift, a kitchen, a warehouse or a fleet. Each one running below what it can handle.

Both start with records and assets you already have. Nothing new to build before the first test.

Size it from your own books. The accounts that went quiet, and what each one used to order. The hours your line sits idle, and what you pay to keep it ready.

Capacity and capability

Co-manufacturing and private label

Your open capacity, making someone else’s brand.

A brand needs reliable production and has no plant of its own. We find them. We build the deal on supply, quality, pricing and volume. It has to work for both sides.

First check:
the real cost to deliver and the quality bar. Then the cash it ties up at the volumes they need.
The result you want:
capacity you already pay for, earning under contract.

Capacity and asset leasing

Get paid for what sits idle.

Equipment, space, trucks or skilled labor that another company would pay to use.

First check:
what it earns after the cost and the risk of sharing it.
The result you want:
idle assets that pay their own way.

Licensing

Get paid for what you already know how to do.

A process, formula, brand, certification or know-how. Another company can use it in a market you don’t serve.

First check:
what can be protected, and who would pay to use it.
The result you want:
something you built once, earning where you don’t sell.

Customer relationships

Win-back

Bring back customers you already paid to win.

Old and quiet accounts, sorted by value and by why they left. Each one gets a specific reason to come back. Outreach goes out only the way you approve, and within your consent rules.

First check:
your own order history, compared against a control group where the data allows.
The result you want:
revenue you earned once, coming back before you spend to find new customers.

Referral partnerships

Get recommended by someone your buyer already trusts.

Another business already serves your ideal buyer. We build a deal where they introduce you. Clear terms say who owns the customer and how both sides get paid.

First check:
whether their customers actually need what you sell.
The result you want:
new buyers who arrive already trusting you, without more ad spend.

Co-marketing and bundles

Two products. One buyer. One offer.

Two products the same buyer needs, sold or marketed together.

First check:
whether the bundle is worth more to the buyer than the parts.
The result you want:
their customers become yours, and yours become theirs.

Distribution and access

Distribution and reseller deals

Sell through companies that already sell to your buyer.

Distributors, resellers and sales teams who already reach the buyers you want.

First check:
what each side keeps after the channel takes its cut.
The result you want:
a new route to market, without building a sales force.

Strategic alliances and joint ventures

Build together what neither side can build alone.

Two companies with customers, skills or assets that fit together build something new. Who brings what, who owns what and who earns what are set in writing.

First check:
whether the combined value is big enough to be worth the coordination.
The result you want:
growth through access, not just ownership.

Supplier and buying alliances

Buy better by buying together.

Shared purchasing, new suppliers, or outsourcing work that isn’t core. All to lower the cost of what you already make.

First check:
the savings after the cost of switching.
The result you want:
lower costs where the numbers hold.
