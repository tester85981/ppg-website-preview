Capabilities

Name what you’ve got. We’ll find who wants it.

Every PPG deal starts with something your business already has, or can reach through a partner. Here’s what we build, where each one starts, and the first thing we check.

  • Capacity and capability
  • Customer relationships
  • Distribution and access

The fastest place to start is what you already paid for.

  • Customers who already bought from you.

    You paid to win them once. Some stopped for reasons worth a second look.

  • Capacity you’re already carrying.

    A line, a shift, a kitchen, a warehouse or a fleet running below what it can handle.

Both start with records and assets you already have. Nothing new to build before the first test.

Capacity and capability

Examples of what we pursue. Not past PPG results.

  • Co-manufacturing and private label

    Your open capacity, making someone else’s brand.

    A brand needs reliable production and has no plant of its own. We find them and build the deal on supply, quality, pricing and volume, so it works for both sides.

    First check: the real cost to deliver, the quality bar, and the cash it ties up at the volumes they need.

    The result you want: capacity you already pay for, earning under contract.

  • Capacity and asset leasing

    Get paid for what sits idle.

    Equipment, space, trucks or skilled labor that another company would pay to use.

    First check: what it earns after the cost and the risk of sharing it.

    The result you want: idle assets that pay their own way.

  • Licensing

    Get paid for what you already know how to do.

    A process, formula, brand, certification or know-how another company could use in a market you don’t serve.

    First check: what can be protected, and who would pay to use it.

    The result you want: something you built once, earning where you don’t sell.

Customer relationships

  • Win-back

    Bring back customers you already paid to win.

    Old and quiet accounts, sorted by value and by why they left, each given a specific reason to come back. Outreach only goes out the way you approve and within your consent rules.

    First check: your own order history, compared against a control group where the data allows.

    The result you want: revenue you earned once, coming back before you spend to find new customers.

  • Referral partnerships

    Get recommended by someone your buyer already trusts.

    Another business already serves your ideal buyer. We build a deal where they introduce you, with clear terms on who owns the customer and how both sides get paid.

    First check: whether their customers actually need what you sell.

    The result you want: new buyers who arrive already trusting you, without more ad spend.

  • Co-marketing and bundles

    Two products. One buyer. One offer.

    Two products the same buyer needs, sold or marketed together.

    First check: whether the bundle is worth more to the buyer than the parts.

    The result you want: their customers become yours, and yours become theirs.

Distribution and access

  • Distribution and reseller deals

    Sell through companies that already sell to your buyer.

    Distributors, resellers and sales teams who already reach the buyers you want.

    First check: what each side keeps after the channel takes its cut.

    The result you want: a new route to market, without building a sales force.

  • Strategic alliances and joint ventures

    Build together what neither side can build alone.

    Two companies with customers, skills or assets that fit together build something new, with who brings what, who owns what and who earns what set in writing.

    First check: whether the combined value is big enough to be worth the coordination.

    The result you want: growth through access, not just ownership.

  • Supplier and buying alliances

    Buy better by buying together.

    Shared purchasing, new suppliers, or outsourcing work that isn’t core, to lower the cost of what you already make.

    First check: the savings after the cost of switching.

    The result you want: lower costs where the numbers hold.
