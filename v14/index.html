The Opportunity That Keeps Getting Pushed Back

The Cost of a Problem Nobody Owns

You can see what needs to happen. Getting it done is another matter.

Maybe you’ve got a production line that doesn’t run at full capacity. Maybe a former customer base nobody has worked in a year. Maybe a company down the road sells to exactly the buyers you want, but nobody has built a relationship with them.

The possibilities are not necessarily mysterious. They are sitting in plain sight.

The problem is ownership.

Sales has a number to hit. Operations has orders to fulfill. Finance is watching cash. You have a company to run.

That leaves the opportunity sitting between departments. Everybody knows it deserves attention. Nobody has enough room to own it.

Another quarter passes. The capacity is still idle. The relationship is still untouched. The new channel is still a conversation from last year.

Internal Bandwidth

You shouldn’t have to make a permanent overhead decision before knowing whether the opportunity is worth pursuing.

This Is Where PPG Comes In

Illustrative Commercial Situation

A Production Line Is Idle. Another Company Needs Production. Is There a Deal?

Hypothetical Example · Not a PPG Result

Imagine a manufacturer with unused capacity and an established brand whose demand has outgrown its current supplier.

An introduction alone doesn’t solve the problem. The manufacturer needs to know whether the extra orders cover materials, labor, quality controls, freight, working capital, and the effect on existing customers. The brand needs confidence in price, quality, supply continuity, and accountability.

The commercial opportunity is to test whether these two businesses can create more contribution together than they can separately. If the numbers and operating conditions hold, the next work is to define the supply terms, align responsibilities, secure approvals, and get the arrangement running.

Direct Answers Before We Speak

What You May Be Wondering.

What Exactly Does PPG Do?

We investigate a defined commercial opportunity, test its economics, select an appropriate structure, originate parties where needed, and undertake the execution work we agree to handle.

Do We Need to Know What Type of Deal We Want?

No. Most owners start with a situation, not a structure. We diagnose first; the mechanism follows the facts, whether that turns out to be a distribution arrangement, a capacity agreement, a partnership, or no deal at all.

What Do You Mean by Performance-Based?

We negotiate participation in the defined economic value of a commercial initiative, such as a share of incremental contribution profit from collected revenue after agreed costs. It is not a guarantee of results, and any client-side legal, capital, or operating costs are disclosed and approved separately.

Do We Give Up Control?

No. No material outside commitment is made in your company’s name without the required approval. You keep decision authority over counterparties, terms, pricing, and your brand.

What Happens After I Contact You?

Ryan reviews the situation personally. If there is a fit, the next step is a working conversation and a written Opportunity Review: the opportunity hypothesis, the material unknowns, and whether further work is warranted. There is no fee and no obligation.
