How an opportunity becomes a decision

A clear role for PPG. A clear decision for you.

Your opportunity needs commercial work, realistic economics and responsibilities that someone can actually deliver.

The PPG Deal Method sets out the work in six steps. The depth and scope depend on the situation. You approve commitments for your business; PPG is responsible for its agreed work.

Know what happens next and what it needs from you.

1. Opportunity Review

Begin with a non-confidential outline. Ryan reviews the fit and, for suitable situations, explores the opportunity with you in a working conversation.

You receive a preliminary written assessment: the commercial possibility, who might want it, an initial structure, assumptions, information gaps and the recommended next decision. It is not a secured partner, demand validation or a final business case.

2. Written engagement

If both sides want to proceed, agree the scope before further development work begins. Define deliverables, responsibilities, compensation, approval requirements, information handling, reporting, duration and how the engagement ends.

Identify client costs, operational contributions and any capital required. A no-fee Review is separate from the commitments involved in pursuing a deal.

3. Business case

Examine what has to be true for the opportunity to work. Demand, pricing, capacity, delivery, timing, costs and cash collection all belong in the assessment.

Separate known facts from estimates. Agree what needs testing and what would make the opportunity unattractive before committing further resources.

4. Partner development

Where the opportunity requires another business, research who might benefit and why. Consider what each side contributes and what could make the arrangement difficult.

Approve the intended approach before outreach in your name. Access to a potential partner is a beginning; willingness and fit still need to be established.

5. Commercial agreement

Develop the practical exchange: contributions, pricing or value sharing, delivery, approvals, reporting, attribution and the arrangements for change or exit.

Your business approves its commitments. Appropriate legal, accounting and other specialist advisers handle the work that requires their expertise or authority.

6. Agreed execution and review

Assign the launch tasks and operating responsibilities. PPG coordinates the work included in its scope; your team and the partner deliver their agreed contributions.

Review actual activity, relevant costs and collected value. Decide what to maintain, improve or stop so that the arrangement is judged on what it produces.

You retain control. The work still needs your business.

review matters within their remit. PPG does not replace legal, tax, accounting, financing or regulated transaction advice.

An approval is not the same as doing all the administration. Equally, a performance arrangement does not remove the need for your team’s time and delivery capability.

Define the value before defining PPG’s share.

The Opportunity Review has no fee. An engagement under this offer has no PPG upfront development fee or retainer. PPG commits only the development resources it has agreed to provide.

Where compensation is a share of incremental profit, the written terms define the relevant collected income, agreed costs, baseline, attribution and payment calculation. Revenue alone does not establish the profit available to share.

A savings arrangement needs a defensible baseline and a definition of net savings. Licensing, recurring participation or a joint venture require terms appropriate to that structure. No percentage or standard deal size is promised on this website.

The agreement also needs to address reporting, adjustments, refunds or reversals, duration and termination. Participation applies to the opportunity and rights actually agreed, rather than an undefined claim on unrelated future business.

Your own approved costs and commitments remain yours. PPG’s performance compensation is not a guarantee that you will incur no cost or achieve a return.

Questions to resolve before going further

What happens if the opportunity does not look worthwhile?

The assessment can recommend waiting or stopping. A written reason is more useful than developing a weak idea because it sounds exciting.

What if we already know a possible partner?

Start with the commercial gap. You may need an introduction, but you may instead need an economic case, an agreement or someone coordinating the agreed development work. The scope should reflect that.

Will PPG contact customers or partners without approval?

Outreach in your business’s name requires your approval. Agree the relevant contacts, approach and message before it begins. Do not send sensitive lists in the initial inquiry.

How much time will our team need?

The initial request is short. A full engagement needs information, access, approvals and delivery input. The amount depends on the opportunity and should be agreed before the work proceeds.

Who decides whether we do a deal?

Your business approves its own commitments. PPG develops and assesses the opportunity within the agreed scope; it cannot commit your company on your behalf.

Can PPG guarantee a partner or a result?

No. Commercial demand, another party’s willingness and delivery performance must be established. PPG can agree its work, reporting and compensation terms; it cannot guarantee another business’s decision or the commercial outcome.

Can we stop?

There is no obligation to proceed beyond the Review. For an engagement, agree the notice, termination, outstanding commitments and any continuing rights before signing.

Is the first request confidential?

Keep it non-confidential. Agree the appropriate information-handling arrangements before sharing sensitive records, contracts or financial details. Read the Privacy notice for how inquiry information is handled.

Is this a brokerage or capital-raising service?

This offer covers commercial opportunity development. It excludes capital raising, company sales and work requiring a licence PPG does not hold.
