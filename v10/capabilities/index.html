Where an opportunity can start

What could your business put to better commercial use?

Start with the capacity, relationships, capabilities or access you already have. Then ask who needs it, what they would contribute and whether the economics work.

These nine opportunity types are starting points for investigation. They are not past PPG results or a promise that every business has a viable deal.

Capacity and capabilities

1. Available capacity and underused assets

Objective: create a profitable use for resources that can reliably serve additional demand.

Illustrative opportunity: suitable production time used to make a product for another brand. This is not a past PPG result.

First check:
available capacity, real demand, quality requirements, incremental cost and any effect on existing customers.

2. Licensing and private-label arrangements

Objective: earn from a capability, product, process or brand through an appropriate commercial arrangement.

Illustrative opportunity: an owner licenses defined intellectual property or supplies a product under another business’s brand. This is not a past PPG result.

First check:
ownership and permitted use, buyer demand, support requirements, quality control and the economics. Rights, approvals and certifications must be verified; they are not automatically transferable.

3. Shared resources and cost arrangements

Objective: achieve sustainable net savings or useful access through a compatible business.

Illustrative opportunity: businesses share a suitable resource or negotiate a joint purchasing arrangement. This is not a past PPG result.

First check:
switching and coordination costs, service quality, commitments, confidentiality and the net benefit after implementation.

Customer relationships

4. Dormant customer and stalled-opportunity recovery

Objective: identify which past accounts or stalled opportunities are worth pursuing again.

Illustrative opportunity: a defined reactivation test using approved communications and a relevant reason to re-engage. This is not a past PPG result.

First check:
why activity stopped, record quality, permissions, current fit, contribution margin and how incremental results will be distinguished from business that would have returned anyway.

5. Complementary offers and cross-selling

Objective: serve a relevant customer need with an offer your business or a compatible partner can deliver.

Illustrative opportunity: an established supplier introduces a complementary service to suitable customers. This is not a past PPG result.

First check:
actual customer need, trust, fulfillment, consent, economic terms and whether the offer creates distraction or dissatisfaction.

6. Multi-party commercial combinations

Objective: combine contributions that create a useful offer no participant can readily deliver alone.

Illustrative opportunity: a supplier, an operator and a distribution partner develop a defined combined offer. This is not a past PPG result.

First check:
the customer’s need, each party’s contribution, decision rights, delivery dependencies and how value and risk are allocated.

Distribution and access

7. Joint ventures and strategic alliances

Objective: develop a defined commercial opportunity around complementary strengths.

Illustrative opportunity: two businesses combine capabilities and access to serve an identified market need. This is not a past PPG result.

First check:
demand, mutual incentives, contributions, governance, delivery and a workable route to exit or change the arrangement.

8. Distribution and referral channels

Objective: reach suitable buyers through a business that already serves them.

Illustrative opportunity: a complementary supplier introduces or distributes an approved offer under defined terms. This is not a past PPG result.

First check:
buyer fit, motivation, channel conflict, economics, customer ownership, reporting and approved communications.

9. Geographic and market expansion

Objective: assess whether an existing capability can reach a new market through the right commercial partner.

Illustrative opportunity: a business works with an appropriate local distributor rather than immediately establishing its own operation. This is not a past PPG result.

First check:
demand, adaptation, local requirements, partner capability, delivery costs and the economics of serving the market.

Choose the opportunity your business can support.

An attractive idea needs more than an underused resource. It needs demand, an acceptable return, a practical delivery route and people able to make the necessary decisions.

The Review helps frame one situation and identify what needs to be known next. It does not value your whole business or confirm a market from a brief description.

Which starting point sounds closest to your situation?

Describe what you have, what is getting in the way and what would make the opportunity worthwhile. If you are an adviser or a prospective partner, explain the role you have in the situation.
