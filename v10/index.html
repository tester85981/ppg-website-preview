Make the next decision before making the next commitment.

  1. Review the situation. Start with an outline. If there is a fit, explore the opportunity and receive a preliminary written assessment.

  2. Agree the engagement. Define the opportunity, responsibilities, compensation, approvals and exit terms before further work.

  3. Check the economics. Examine demand assumptions, delivery requirements, relevant costs and the return each side needs.

  4. Develop the partner opportunity. Where a partner is needed, research the fit and seek approval before outreach in your name.

  5. Build the agreement. Resolve the commercial terms and use the appropriate advisers for legal, financial or regulated work.

  6. Coordinate the agreed execution. Assign responsibilities, track what happens and review whether to continue, change or stop.

You contribute business knowledge and delivery decisions. PPG coordinates its agreed development work so that you can assess progress without taking on every task yourself.

See who does what

Judge the opportunity by what has to be true.

Who would buy or participate? Why would they choose this arrangement? What would it cost to deliver? What could prevent it from working?

Your assessment should make those questions easier to answer. It should also make the gaps visible.

The Opportunity Review is a preliminary assessment. A partner’s willingness, market demand and final economics still need to be established. If the case is weak, the recommendation can be to wait or stop.

There is no fee for the Opportunity Review and no obligation to continue.

For an agreed engagement, PPG’s compensation is defined around the value the particular opportunity creates. For profit-sharing arrangements, the agreement specifies the relevant collected income and costs before the share is calculated.
