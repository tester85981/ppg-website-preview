An agreement is useful when the business behind it works.

A partner introduction is a beginning. A signed document is another step. The arrangement still needs demand, viable economics and people who can deliver.

Your interests belong in the decision.

You should understand what is known, what is assumed and what could make the opportunity unattractive. That includes the other side’s incentives: a partner needs a worthwhile reason to participate and keep delivering.

If a situation is outside PPG’s role or the case does not justify further work, the useful recommendation is to say so.

You know who is responsible for PPG’s work.

You can ask about the reasoning behind an opportunity, the work proposed and the limits of the engagement. Founder involvement gives you a named person to work with; it does not replace the need to assess the evidence and the terms.

Judge the work on its substance.

Look for a clear opportunity, visible assumptions, a credible value exchange, defined responsibilities and an economic case worth testing.

The examples on this website illustrate types of opportunity. They are not client case studies or reported PPG results. The Opportunity Review is a preliminary assessment rather than a guarantee of a partner or an outcome.

Illustrative Opportunity Review specimen

Situation:
A manufacturer reports available production time and an interest in supplying complementary brands.
Commercial hypothesis:
A suitable brand may need the manufacturer’s capability without establishing production itself.
Possible exchange:
The manufacturer contributes verified production capability. The brand contributes demand, specifications and the responsibilities agreed for sales and distribution.
Known facts:
None independently established in this specimen. The starting situation is hypothetical.
Information required:
Usable capacity; quality and approval requirements; unit economics; order and payment terms; working capital; effect on current customers; possible buyer demand.
Initial structure to investigate:
A defined supply or private-label arrangement, subject to fit and specialist review.
What could defeat it:
Insufficient demand, unsuitable margins, delivery constraints, quality requirements or unacceptable cash exposure.
Recommended next decision:
Establish delivery economics and buyer requirements before approaching a shortlist under an approved message.
Client benefit of this assessment:
A narrower decision about what to investigate next. It does not establish that an opportunity is commercially viable.
