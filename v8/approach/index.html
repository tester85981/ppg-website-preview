The PPG Deal Method

Six steps. A clear decision at every one.

At each step, you look at the facts with us and decide how to move forward. If demand, the numbers or the ability to deliver change, your written agreement says what happens next.

Steps 1 to 5 cost you nothing. PPG carries that work up to the close.

1. Opportunity Review

What you get:
a straight answer on whether there’s a real opportunity here.
What we do:
read your outline. Hold one working session. Give you a written answer with our recommendation.
What we need from you:
a few sentences, one session, and what only you know.
The decision:
is it real? Do the numbers work? Is there a clear way to get it done?

2. Terms first

What you get:
one written agreement, before any work starts.
What we put in it:
  • the opportunity and the scope
  • who does what
  • how PPG is paid out of what the deal creates, after you profit
  • how results are measured, where that matters
  • approvals, confidentiality and reporting
  • any costs on your side, named up front
  • how long it runs and how it ends
What we need from you:
who decides what, and the information we need to size it up.
The decision:
do both sides approve the agreement? Nothing starts until they do.

3. Check the math

What you get:
the real numbers before anyone gets excited.
What we do:
start with your own numbers, what the asset costs you to carry today. Then map who would want it and what it takes to deliver. Then test the numbers and name what has to be true.
What we need from you:
access to the information and people the work needs.
The decision:
is there a real business case worth taking to a deal?

4. Find the partner

What you get:
a partner with a real reason to say yes.
What we do:
find the companies that need what you have. Research each one, make the case and qualify the fit. Make contact only the way you approve.
What we need from you:
your OK on every message and every target list. Nothing goes out in your name before that. And your time for the conversations that matter.
The decision:
is there a partner with a real reason to say yes?

5. Build the deal

What you get:
terms both sides want to keep.
What we do:
design what each side puts in and gets back. Pricing and revenue share. Who does what, and when. Exclusivity and risk. Credit for results and reporting. We lead or support the negotiation, as agreed.
What we need from you:
your approval of the terms, and your signature. Only yours.
The decision:
is this a deal both sides would want to grow?

6. Make it run

What you get:
the deal live, and tracked against what we agreed.
What we do:
get it live, clear the friction and track progress.
What we need from you:
deliver what the partnership sells.
The decision:
is it hitting the goal? Keep going, adjust or exit under the agreement.

We carry the work. You keep the decisions.

The Profit-First Partnership

Every PPG engagement runs on performance. That is the basis of every deal we take on.

Working with us costs you nothing. No fee to start. No retainer while we hunt.

PPG carries the cost and the resources up to the close. The research. The outreach. Building the deal. The negotiation.

PPG is paid only out of the new value a closed deal creates. Your costs on that deal get covered first. Then you make a solid profit. Then PPG gets paid.

If no deal closes, PPG earns nothing.

Some deals carry costs on your end, inside your own operation. When one does, your terms name those costs up front. You never discover them later.

The share itself is set for each deal. It can be a portion of revenue or margin, royalties, or a mix. The right one depends on the opportunity, the work, the risk and what each side brings.

Your written agreement puts it in plain language before anyone commits. The split. What counts as revenue. What each side owes. How either side can end it.

It also sets who does what and who decides what. It sets how results are measured and credited, where that matters. And it sets how long it runs.

The promise. If a deal doesn’t create new value, PPG doesn’t get paid. You profit first.

Why we can work this way. We’re paid out of the value a closed deal creates. So we only take deals where the math works for you first. If we’re wrong about the opportunity, the cost is ours.

Incentives drive behavior. Ours included. So ours point at one thing: a deal that pays you first.

The terms have to work for you, your partner and PPG. If they don’t, there’s no deal.

There’s no fee for the Review. Here are the other straight answers.

Are you consultants?

No. A consultant hands you a plan and leaves the work to you. If you’ve paid for a plan before, you know where it ends up. We do the analysis too. Then we find the partner, build the deal, negotiate it and make it run. You keep every decision.

Are you a broker?

No. An introduction can be part of the work. The work keeps going after it: the terms, the negotiation, the launch and the tracking.

Who is this not for?

Startups. Anyone raising capital, selling a company or brokering an acquisition. Work that needs a license we don’t hold. If that’s you, you hear it straight, before any working session.

Do we need to build a team?

No. We carry the work. Your team gives us the information, approvals and delivery only your business can.

How involved will I be?

At every decision that matters, and nowhere else. You approve. You don’t administer. Before work starts, the agreement spells out three things. What we handle on our own. What needs your approval. How we report.

Will you contact our customers or partners?

Only with your approval, in the way you approve, and within your consent and opt-out rules.

What does the Opportunity Review cost?

There is no fee for the Review.

What does it cost to work with PPG?

Nothing up front. There’s no fee to start and no retainer while we look for the deal. We carry the cost of the research, the outreach, building the deal and the negotiation. We’re paid only out of the new value a closed deal creates. And only after your costs on that deal are covered and you’ve made a solid profit. If no deal closes, we earn nothing. One exception: some deals carry costs inside your own operation. When one does, your terms name them before you commit.

Why would you do the work before you’re paid?

Because we’re paid out of what the deal creates, not out of your budget. That keeps us honest about the math. If a deal won’t pay you, it won’t pay us, so you hear that early.

What if there’s nothing there?

You get it in writing, with the reason. A clear no is worth having.

Can we stop?

Your agreement answers that before work starts. It spells out how long the deal runs and how either side exits.

What about confidential information?

Keep your first outline non-confidential. Before anything sensitive is shared, both sides agree confidentiality terms in writing.
